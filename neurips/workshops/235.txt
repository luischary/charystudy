### Sketch of the Summary

1. **Overview about the paper**: The paper investigates the concept of interpretable approximations in machine learning, specifically focusing on how complex models like deep neural networks can be approximated by simpler models, such as decision trees. It introduces a theoretical framework to classify the conditions under which a target concept can be approximated by a simpler hypothesis class.

2. **Main contributions**: The authors present a trichotomy that categorizes the relationship between a target concept and a hypothesis class based on their approximability and interpretability. They also provide algebraic characterizations of these concepts and extend their findings to various complexity measures.

3. **Proposed technique**: The paper introduces the notion of interpretable approximations, defining conditions under which a complex concept can be approximated by a simpler class of hypotheses. The authors utilize decision trees as a measure of complexity and establish a framework for understanding the depth of these trees in relation to accuracy.

4. **Main results**: The authors demonstrate that for any given target concept and hypothesis class, one of three scenarios will hold: (i) the concept cannot be approximated, (ii) it can be approximated but not interpreted, or (iii) it can be uniformly interpreted with a constant depth. They also provide bounds on the complexity of approximations.

5. **Applications or possible practical applications**: The findings have implications for fields requiring interpretable AI, such as healthcare and law enforcement, where understanding model decisions is crucial. The theoretical framework can guide the development of interpretable models that maintain high accuracy.
