### Sketch of the Summary

1. **Overview about the paper**: The paper introduces a new metric called Adversarial Compression Ratio (ACR) to assess memorization in large language models (LLMs). It discusses the implications of LLMs memorizing training data versus generalizing from it, particularly in the context of legal compliance and data usage.

2. **Main contributions**: The paper proposes a novel definition of memorization based on compression, introduces the MINIPROMPT algorithm for practical implementation, and demonstrates the effectiveness of ACR in evaluating memorization compared to existing definitions.

3. **Proposed technique**: The ACR metric measures how much shorter a prompt can be compared to the output it elicits from the model. If a shorter prompt can reproduce a longer training string, the string is considered memorized. The MINIPROMPT algorithm is used to find these minimal prompts.

4. **Main results**: The authors validate their approach through various experiments, showing that ACR effectively identifies memorized data in LLMs, even in cases where traditional methods fail. They also demonstrate that larger models tend to memorize more data.

5. **Applications or possible practical applications**: The findings have implications for legal compliance regarding data usage in LLMs, providing a tool for model owners to assess and ensure they are not violating copyright laws. The method can also be used in monitoring unlearning processes in models.
